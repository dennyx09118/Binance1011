# **The Crypto in Your Binance Account May Not Fully Belong to You: A Breakdown of Binance's Asset Control Framework**

Many users assume that the assets shown in their Binance account belong entirely to them.

But within Binance's Terms of Use, two provisions make that assumption far less certain.

-----
**The First Concept: A First-Priority Security Interest**

Clause 11.1.1(a) states that Binance holds a:

***"continuing first-priority security interest"***

over the assets in your account.

The operative word is **"first-priority."**

In legal terms, this means that if a dispute, claim, or asset allocation ever arises, the platform's secured interest may rank ahead of yours.

In other words: when conflicts emerge between you and the platform, the agreement grants the platform significant legal standing over those assets — before any questions have been resolved.

-----
**The Second Concept: Absolute Assignment**

Even more significant is another provision within the same clause.

The agreement states that users:

***"assign and agree to assign, with full title guarantee, the Assigned Rights to NCCL...absolutely."***

The critical word is **"absolutely."**

So what are the "Assigned Rights"?

The agreement defines them as:

***"all rights relating to the Agreement, the Secured Property or any Transaction...which you may have now or in the future against any ADGM Binance Entity or any third party."***

Let that sink in:

**Rights you currently hold may be assigned.** **Rights you may acquire in the future may also be assigned.** And those rights extend not just to claims against Binance — but potentially to rights involving third parties as well.

This goes well beyond a platform "holding assets on your behalf."

From a legal standpoint, users are surrendering substantial rights connected to those assets before any dispute has even occurred.

-----
**Invisible During Normal Times — Decisive During a Crisis**

Most of the time, these provisions stay out of sight.

The real stakes only emerge under extreme conditions:

- A liquidity crisis
- A major legal dispute
- A platform-wide risk event
- Insolvency or restructuring

When that happens, the question becomes: **who has priority over the assets?**

A more honest framing may be this:

You are not simply "depositing crypto."

You are placing assets into a legal and operational structure where your position may resemble that of an unsecured creditor — one of the parties standing furthest back in the repayment queue.

-----
**The Lesson Many Users Learned From FTX**

After the collapse of FTX, many users encountered this reality for the first time.

Assets displayed in their accounts were not automatically treated as property fully separated from the bankruptcy estate.

Many users instead found themselves classified as unsecured creditors — joining a lengthy claims process.

Most of the time, this legal structure remains invisible.

But once triggered, its consequences can be decisive.

-----
**What Can the Platform Actually Do?**

**Three Layers of Authorization**

Clause 11.1.4 states that the platform may, without prior notice, dispose of assets in a user's account if it considers doing so appropriate:

***"without notice to you...in its sole and absolute discretion...appropriate or sell some or all of the Secured Property in the manner and on the terms and price it deems appropriate."***

Several elements stand out:

- No advance notice required
- Sole and absolute discretion
- Terms set by the platform
- Price set by the platform

The agreement grants broad authority over how and when such actions may be taken.

-----
**Cross-Account, Cross-Asset Deductions**

Clause 11.1.6 goes further still.

It grants rights that apply:

***"irrespective of the currency or type of asset, place of payment or delivery"***

This means the agreement is not confined to a specific cryptocurrency, a specific account, or a specific location.

If the platform determines that obligations are owed, it may act across different asset types and currencies — with exchange rates determined according to its own procedures.

-----
**The "No Rehypothecation" Clause — and the Exception**

Clause 8.5 is titled **"No Rehypothecation."**

At first glance, it sounds reassuring.

It states that client assets will not be rehypothecated.

But an exception follows:

***"Client Assets held for you may temporarily be used for the benefit of NCCL or one or more of our other clients in the course of settlement processes."***

Assets held for a client may be temporarily used — during settlement activities — for the benefit of NCCL or other clients.

The agreement does not define what "temporarily" means.

-----
**Not Isolated Clauses — A Complete Control Framework**

These are not three unrelated provisions buried in fine print.

Taken together, they form a coherent framework governing control, priority, and treatment of assets within the platform's legal structure.

And users authorize that entire framework the moment they click:

**"I Agree."**
