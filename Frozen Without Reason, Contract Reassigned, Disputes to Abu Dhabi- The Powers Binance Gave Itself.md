# **Frozen Without Reason, Contract Reassigned, Disputes to Abu Dhabi: The Powers Binance Gave Itself**

The answer has been in the agreement all along.

-----
**Your Account Can Be Restricted Without Being Given a Reason**

Under Clause 25, Binance reserves the right to restrict, suspend, or terminate access to your account at any time.

The triggering conditions span nearly an entire page.

They range from:

**"reasonable suspicion"**

to situations where Binance believes there are:

**"other valid reasons"**

Together, these provisions cover an exceptionally broad range of circumstances.

But the most important language appears in Clause 25.2(b):

***"our decision...may be based on confidential criteria that are essential to our risk management and security protocols. You agree that we are under no obligation to disclose the details of our risk management and security procedures to you."***

In plain terms:

The reason your account was restricted may be classified.

And Binance states it has no obligation to tell you what that reason is.

You ask why.

The answer may simply be:

**"We cannot disclose that information."**

You want to prove your innocence.

But you are not told what you are being accused of.

You are expected to respond to criteria that remain entirely invisible to you.

In a situation where all information sits on one side, the platform may legally continue restricting access to your assets while its review process runs its course.

This is not a procedural loophole.

It is authority explicitly written into the agreement.

-----
**Your Contract Counterparty Can Change Without Your Approval**

According to Clause 40.5, Binance may transfer its rights and obligations under the agreement to another entity at any time.

The agreement states:

***"we may assign or transfer any of our rights or obligations under the Agreement at any time to anyone else...You hereby irrevocably consent, in advance and without further action by you, to any such novation."***

"Novation" is a legal term for the transfer of a contract — including all its rights and obligations — to a new party. In effect, your original counterparty is replaced by someone else, and the agreement continues under the new entity.

The critical word here is **"irrevocably."**

You consented to this at the moment of registration.

Before anything happened.

And according to the agreement, that consent cannot later be withdrawn.

In practice:

You may sign an agreement with Company A today.

Tomorrow, that agreement may be transferred to Company B.

If your account is frozen and you want to file a complaint, challenge a decision, or pursue legal action, you may first need to answer a more basic question:

**Who is actually your counterparty now?**

-----
**Even If You Decide to Fight Back, There Is Another Barrier**

And this may be the steepest one.

**Step One: Internal Resolution**

Under the agreement, users must first exhaust Binance's internal dispute-resolution process before proceeding further.

This preliminary process can last up to **170 days**.

Nearly six months of waiting — before you are even eligible to take the next step.

**Step Two: Arbitration in Abu Dhabi**

Clause 37.3 states:

***"The seat of arbitration shall be the ADGM."***

All disputes must be resolved through arbitration based in the Abu Dhabi Global Market.

For most ordinary users, that immediately raises a set of serious practical challenges:

- International legal procedures
- Specialized arbitration counsel familiar with ICC rules
- Significant legal costs
- Cross-border participation

For many people, the cost of pursuing the claim may exceed the value of the frozen assets themselves.

-----
**The Path Narrows Further**

Clause 37.11 states:

***"you and Binance irrevocably waive any right to commence proceedings in any UAE court."***

Users are not simply required to use arbitration in Abu Dhabi.

They also give up the right to bring any case before UAE courts.

The options available to users are narrowed at every turn.

-----
**A Point Many Users May Overlook**

Under Article 497 of the Civil Code of the People's Republic of China, standard-form clauses that significantly exempt one party from liability or strip the other party of major rights may be considered unenforceable under Chinese legal principles.

However, Clause 37.9 states:

***"This arbitration agreement shall be exclusively governed by, and construed in accordance with, the laws of the ADGM."***

In other words: the legal protections users may be familiar with under their own legal systems may have little bearing on the dispute-resolution framework specified in this agreement.

The law may exist.

But the agreement places the dispute within a legal structure that many users will find difficult to access, navigate, or practically use.

The path to enforcement can itself become the obstacle.

-----
**Looking at the Structure as a Whole**

An account can be restricted without any disclosed reason.

The contractual counterparty can change without your active consent.

The cost and complexity of pursuing a dispute can be high enough that many users walk away before the process even begins.

Taken individually, each of these provisions can be explained as a standard risk-management or legal mechanism.

Taken together, they point toward the same underlying reality:

**A system in which challenging the platform becomes extraordinarily difficult.**
