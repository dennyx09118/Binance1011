# **You Know the Rules Now. This Is the Fight We Are Already In: 10/11 Collective Arbitration Has Begun**

This series started with a 73-page agreement.

You have now read what is inside it.

So — what comes next?

Three things worth doing.

-----
**One: Stop Treating an Exchange Like a Bank**

Based on the structure of the agreement, an exchange functions more like a **trading venue** than a **custodian**.

You may choose to trade there.

But assets that are not actively being traded do not need to sit there indefinitely.

For long-term holdings, consider moving assets to addresses controlled by your own private keys.

This is not technological purism.

It is basic risk isolation.

You do not need to chase absolute decentralization.

But you should at least know that you have a choice.

-----
**Two: Preserve Evidence From Day One**

Most people start looking for evidence only after something has already gone wrong.

By then, the critical information is already out of reach.

Trading records — on the platform. System logs — on the platform. Customer support conversations — often only within the platform's own systems.

What you can do right now is straightforward:

- Save deposit and withdrawal screenshots
- Record timestamps of important actions
- Archive every customer support exchange

Under normal conditions, these materials feel unnecessary.

In a dispute, they may be the only external evidence you have.

-----
**Three: At Least Know These Clauses Exist**

You do not need to read all 73 pages.

But four provisions are worth keeping in mind:

- **Clause 25** — Account restrictions and suspensions
- **Clauses 33 & 35** — System-failure liability disclaimers
- **Clause 37** — Arbitration in Abu Dhabi
- **Clause 11** — Rights and interests over account assets

You do not need to memorize them.

You just need to know they are there.

Most risks don't materialize because people know about them.

They materialize because people assume they never will.
