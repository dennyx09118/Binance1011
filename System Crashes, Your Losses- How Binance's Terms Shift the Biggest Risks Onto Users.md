# **System Crashes, Your Losses: How Binance's Terms Shift the Biggest Risks Onto Users**
Extreme market volatility. System outages. Forced liquidations.

When everything collapses in seconds — who is actually responsible?

Was it your own delayed reaction? Or was the platform itself malfunctioning?

And if the system was the problem — is the platform required to make you whole?

The answer is written into the agreement. Most users were simply never told to read it.

-----
Open Binance's Terms of Use, Clause 33.2:

***"Binance shall not in any event be liable for any system error, faults or failure of the Binance Platform whatsoever and howsoever arising or caused."***

In plain English: no matter how the system fails, freezes, or breaks down, Binance states it bears no responsibility for the losses that follow.

Pay close attention to those words:

***"whatsoever and howsoever"***

In legal drafting, this is one of the broadest liability disclaimers possible.

Regardless of cause. Regardless of circumstance. The outcome is the same:

**No compensation.**

This is not vague language. This is not a gray area.

It is an explicit liability waiver written directly into the contract you agreed to when you signed up.

-----
The extreme volatility event on October 11 of last year became a real-world test of how this clause operates:

Millions of positions were liquidated. Many users had no meaningful window to react within those critical minutes.

The platform's own systems became part of the risk — and yet the agreement had already decided in advance who would bear the consequences.

-----
**The First Category: System Failures**

Outages. Lag. Execution delays.

Clause 33.10 explicitly lists:

***"errors, malfunctions, breakdowns, inability to access, faults, interruptions or delays"***

If your losses occurred during any of the above — system errors, crashes, service outages, or latency spikes — the platform states it is not responsible.

Under the logic of this agreement, that portion of the risk belongs entirely to you.

-----
**The Second Category: Third-Party and Network Issues**

This one is easy to overlook.

Clause 35.2 extends the disclaimer further:

***"...any technical problems, system failures, malfunctions, communication line failures, high internet traffic or demand...or any similar technical problems or defects experienced."***

This covers:

- Internet congestion
- Communication line failures
- Traffic overload during market volatility
- Broadly defined "similar technical disruptions"

The logic is straightforward:

Your connection drops — your problem. An API breaks — your problem. The network buckles under extreme market pressure — still your problem.

Not Binance's.

-----
**The Third Category — and the Most Consequential: "Force Majeure"**

Most people picture force majeure as earthquakes, wars, or major natural disasters.

But in practice, the scope defined in these agreements tends to be far broader.

Depending on interpretation, it may extend to:

- Abnormal market volatility
- System overload under heavy load
- Shifts in the technical environment

The irony is hard to miss: the moments when users need the platform most are often the exact moments when the platform claims the least responsibility.

-----
**The One Exception**

Clause 33.10 does include a final carve-out.

According to the agreement, there is essentially only one path around these disclaimers:

***"...caused by fraud or wilful default of Binance (as determined by a court of competent jurisdiction)..."***

In other words: unless you can prove in a court of law that Binance acted intentionally or fraudulently, these disclaimers will generally hold.

-----
I previously held a position within the core management structure of a major exchange. I am now assisting multiple affected users in pursuing their rights.

I've seen how these systems work from the inside. I've also seen what users face when they try to push back.

There are typically three walls.

**Wall One: Evidence**

You must prove the loss was caused by the platform — not by your own trading decisions.

But nearly all critical evidence is held exclusively by the platform: trading records, system logs, order timestamps, backend operational data.

Users have no independent means to access or verify any of it.

**Wall Two: Time**

The complaint and appeals process can be lengthy.

While you wait, your assets remain in limbo. Meanwhile, the agreement imposes procedural requirements that must be completed before formal dispute resolution can even begin.

**Wall Three: Cost**

If the dispute escalates to arbitration, the designated venue is Abu Dhabi under ICC international arbitration rules.

For most ordinary users, the cost of getting there may easily exceed the value of what was lost.

-----
In the end, users may not only be left bearing the losses — they may also find it nearly impossible to prove those losses weren't their own fault.

**The risk was theirs. The terms made sure of it.**
