## II. The Black Box of 10.11: Silence Beyond Compensation
Let’s go back to that number: **$28 billion**.

On October 11, 2025, what was flashing on the screen wasn’t just price—\
it was the life savings of countless families evaporating in real time.

**1.6 million accounts were wiped out within hours.**

Binance later allocated **$283 million** for compensation.\
In the official statement, this was framed as “putting users first.”

But against $28 billion in liquidations,\
this looks more like **a 1% pittance to silence the crowd.**

So what about the remaining **$27.7 billion**?

Some say it was extreme market conditions.\
Some say it was a system failure.\
Others raise more complex possibilities—\
**was there a liquidity game that amplified the impact?**

As Jane Street resurfaced in public discussions due to the Terra collapse case,\
this Wall Street market maker was once again pushed into the spotlight.

People began revisiting the so-called **“10 AM effect,”**\
questioning whether algorithm-driven liquidity shocks may have played a role under extreme conditions.

There is no definitive evidence pointing to anyone.\
**But the questions haven’t gone away.**

Whose money was it?\
How did it disappear?\
Why did it vanish within hours?

**Four months have passed.**

We haven’t seen a full post-mortem.\
We haven’t seen transparent on-chain data.\
What we’ve seen instead are bigger and bigger narratives.

In an industry that prides itself on decentralization,\
the most unsettling thing is ironically\
**that impenetrable centralized black box.**

If a system can be overturned in extreme moments,\
then the problem is no longer just about market conditions.

In a bull market, exchanges are comrades.\
In a bear market, they start to look like predators sharpening their knives.

Human nature is like this.\
**Crypto just amplifies it.**
